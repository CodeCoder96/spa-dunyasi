## Two kinds of pedicure furniture

Buyers often use "pedicure chair" and "pedicure spa unit" interchangeably, but they describe slightly different things. A pedicure chair can be any seat where a client rests their feet for a therapist. A pedicure spa unit is a self-contained workstation: the client sits in an elevated chair with an integrated foot spa section in front of it, and the therapist sits facing them.

Our [treatment and pedicure chair range](kategori:bakim-ve-pedikur-koltuklari) includes four pedicure spa units, [Light](urun:SD-033), [Spa Life](urun:SD-035), Spa and [Wood](urun:SD-037), each with a foot spa section. The same range also includes the [Podology care chair](urun:SD-034), an electric chair without a foot spa that serves a different style of foot care. This guide helps you decide which suits your business and which site questions to settle before ordering.

## Match the furniture to your service model

**Nail bars and beauty salons.** Pedicures are often sold together with manicures, and clients value a sociable setting. A pedicure spa unit is the natural fit.

**Hotel and resort spas.** Pedicures usually form part of a package and take place in a quieter room. The look of the unit needs to sit comfortably within the spa's overall interior scheme.

**Clinical foot care and podology.** The client's position changes frequently, and raising the legs or adjusting the chair height matters more than soaking. Here an electric care chair is the more practical tool.

Many operators run more than one model: spa units on the main salon floor and a podology chair in a separate treatment room.

The honest summary is that Light, Spa, Spa Life and Wood share the same catalogue data. Each is listed at 190 cm wide, 140 cm long and 110 cm high. A minimalist city nail studio and a warm, timber-led resort spa are unlikely to pick the same unit.

## Manicure and pedicure at the same time

All four units allow a manicure and a pedicure to be done simultaneously. Commercially, that means two services in one appointment slot and a shorter visit for the client. To make it work in practice:

- leave space beside the chair for a second therapist to sit with their manicure kit;
- plan a work surface alongside; most tables in our [manicure range](kategori:manikur) are 130 cm long, 55 cm wide and 82 cm high;
- for a separate manicure station, the [manicure chair](urun:SD-040) offers clients a hydraulic height mechanism with a 77-92 cm range.

## When the Podology care chair makes more sense

The Podology care chair works on a completely different principle. It has no foot spa section. Instead, height, backrest and leg section are adjusted electrically by remote control, and a fourth motor can be added for the Trendelenburg position. A face hole, heating and a bolster are also available as options.

It is listed at 77 cm wide and 180 cm long, with a load capacity of 200 kg. Because back and leg sections adjust independently, the same chair adapts to different care positions, which makes it a good fit for multi-purpose treatment rooms.

## Site services: water, drainage and power

Plumbing is the item most likely to delay a pedicure installation, so settle it early. We do not quote product-specific technical values here; these are the questions we will work through with you at the quotation stage:

- How will the foot spa section be filled and emptied?
- Is there a water supply and drain close to where the unit will stand, or does the plumbing need rerouting?
- Where will the socket for height adjustment and any other equipment go?
- Is the floor finish suitable for occasional splashes?

On a new-build project, answer these with your architect before placing the order. On a refurbishment, the plumbing may well dictate where the unit goes.

## Layout and the therapist's workspace

Each pedicure spa unit needs room for two people: the client getting in and out, and the therapist sitting in front of the foot spa section. Leave enough space at the front for the therapist's stool and trolley, and enough at the sides for the client to step up comfortably.

Two products complete the workstation:

- **Stool.** Our [stool](urun:SD-062) runs on castors, is listed with a 50-65 cm height range and comes in backed, backless, saddle and flat versions. Because pedicure work involves leaning forward, many therapists prefer a saddle seat.
- **Magnifier lamp.** For detailed nail and foot work, the floor-standing magnifier lamp with 5X magnification and 90 LEDs brings light right to the working point.

## How many stations, and in what arrangement?

Base the number of stations on how demand spreads through the day and how many therapists can work in the room at once. If demand peaks at weekends or in the evening, it can be more efficient to plan for an average day and manage peaks through booking intervals.

With several stations, decide between a side-by-side row and a facing or screened arrangement. A row creates a sociable atmosphere for clients who come in together and keeps plumbing along a single wall. Screened or facing layouts give more privacy. Your client profile and the mood of the space should guide the choice.

## Client comfort beyond the chair

Pedicure clients sit in one place for a long time, so think about the experience from their seat. What will they look at: a wall, a window, the rest of the salon? Where will they put their phone, bag or drink? Is there something to hold when stepping up? Small additions such as a side table, a basket for belongings or a wall shelf often shape the experience as much as the unit itself.

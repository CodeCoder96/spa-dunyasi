## Start with the guest journey, not the shopping list

Fit-out budgets tend to go wrong in the same way: furniture is ordered from a single long list, and only later does someone notice that the relaxation area has too few loungers or that nobody planned a socket for the towel cabinet. A more reliable approach is to walk through the building as a guest would. Arrival, changing, the wet area, the treatment room and finally a quiet place to rest each call for different equipment, and the distance between them affects what goes where.

For each zone, write down three things before you look at products. How many guests will be in that space at the same time? Which services will be offered there? Does it need water, drainage or electricity? Once those answers are on paper, the list almost writes itself, and you can see which items depend on the builder and which can be ordered at any point.

The checklist below follows that route through a typical day spa or hotel spa. It maps each zone to the product groups in the Spa World range, which we supply as an authorised sales point. Treat it as a starting framework and adapt it to your floor plan.

## Treatment rooms

These rooms generate most of the revenue, so plan them first and give each one a defined purpose. A double massage suite, a facial room and a room set aside for Thai massage are furnished quite differently.

Leave a clear walkway on every side for the therapist. The main choice is the lifting arrangement: some models come fixed, with a single motor or with two motors, while others are built with three motors. Questions to answer for every treatment room:
- Will the bed be ordered with the heating option, or will warmth come from another product?
- Do therapists of different heights share the room? If so, consider a model with height adjustment.
- Will products be stored in a built-in drawer or on a separate trolley?

## Relaxation area

After a treatment, guests need somewhere calm to sit or lie down, and in most projects this zone takes up more floor space than expected. Most relaxation loungers in our catalogue are 65 cm wide, and where a backrest height is listed it is 85 cm.

Items to include:
- **Loungers.** Decide between fixed backrests and backrests raised by hand. The [Mercury relaxation lounger](urun:SD-024), for instance, has a low 45 cm frame and a manual backrest that lifts to 35°.
- **Side tables.** A small lounge table beside each lounger, or between two, gives guests somewhere to put down a glass or a phone.
- **Outdoor seating.** Pool terraces and gardens need furniture suited to both indoor and outdoor use, such as the rattan sun lounger. List it separately from indoor loungers.

To set the number of loungers, look at how many treatment rooms finish at roughly the same time. Those guests all arrive in the relaxation area together, and if there is nowhere to sit, the treatment schedule comes under pressure too.

## Towel and hygiene station

This is the zone most often missing from first drafts. A busy spa moves a large volume of towels, robes and peshtemals each day, and that laundry needs a defined place to wait and a route to travel.

- **Towel warmer.** The [towel warmer cabinet](urun:SD-043) has a UV filter and a heating element. Its specification lists a capacity of 100 to 120 towels and a cabinet temperature of 70 °C – 80°C. Put it within easy reach of the treatment rooms.
- **Steriliser cabinet.** Built on the same cabinet design, with different volume options. It completes the station where small tools and towels are kept.

## Accessories that complete each room

Once the main furniture is chosen, the smaller items follow. Individually they look minor; together they are a noticeable share of the budget, and when they are missing the working routine suffers.

## Phasing the order

Not everything has to arrive on the same pallet. Dividing the list into three groups helps you control cash flow without risking the opening.

**Before handover.** Anything tied to construction: wet-area equipment, heat rooms and pedicure units that need plumbing. Adding these later can mean reopening floors, walls or drain runs.

**For opening day.** Beds and chairs for every treatment room, relaxation loungers, the towel warmer and essential accessories. Guests touch these directly, so service cannot start without them.

**Once demand is clear.** A second towel cabinet, extra bolster shapes, additional oil essences or a spare stool can wait until you have seen real occupancy. Holding back here keeps unused stock out of the storeroom.

If you plan to order pieces from the same series at different times, record the timber and upholstery colour references now so that later deliveries match the rooms already in use.

## Final check before you order

Run through these points with your project team:

1. Does every treatment room have a bed or chair, a trolley, a stool and, where needed, a magnifier lamp?
2. Does the number of loungers match the number of rooms that finish together?
3. Is all wet-area equipment shown on the plumbing drawings?
4. Are socket positions fixed for the towel warmer, steriliser and swimsuit dryer?
5. Do the chosen timber and upholstery colours work together across rooms?
6. For products whose dimensions can be adapted, are measured room sizes ready?

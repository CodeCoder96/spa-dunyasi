## Who this guide is for

This guide is written for hotel groups, spa operators, interior designers and purchasing teams who are fitting out a relaxation room and need to specify loungers from a single range. It assumes you already know roughly where the room sits in the guest journey and how many people pass through your thermal suite. What it adds is a way of narrowing our [relaxation lounger range](kategori:dinlenme-yataklari) down to the two or three models that fit your brief, so that the conversation with us can move straight to finishes and quantities.

The range comprises 10 models. Seven pair a moisture-resistant timber frame with leather upholstery, two are built around a marble or glass mosaic body, and one is a rattan piece intended for indoor and outdoor settings.

## Start with the room, not the catalogue

A relaxation lounger is different from a treatment bed in one important way: nobody works on it. The guest sits, reclines and gets up again without help. That shifts the buying criteria away from working height and motors and towards four practical questions.

1. **How wet will guests be?** A room that opens straight off the hammam or steam room will see damp robes and swimwear. A separate quiet lounge further down the corridor will not.
2. **How long do guests stay?** A short pause between thermal sessions calls for something easy to get on and off. A longer stay, perhaps with reading or tea, calls for an adjustable back.
3. **What is the layout?** A single row along a wall, facing pairs, or small clusters around side tables all change how much clearance you need between pieces.
4. **Who are your guests?** Resort hotels with a broad age profile and day spas with a younger clientele can reasonably choose different seat heights.

Write the answers down before you open any product page. They will rule out half the range on their own.

## Dimensions that affect the floor plan

The loungers are close in size, but the small differences add up across a full room.

**Width.** Most models are 65 cm wide. [Odessa](urun:SD-028) is the exception at 75 cm, which gives a roomier surface but means fewer units along the same wall.

**Length.** Evony, Light, Uranus and Venus are 195 cm long; Mercury, Moon, Odessa, Marble and Mosaic are 200 cm. The difference rarely decides a purchase on its own, though it matters when you are trying to keep a clear walkway in front of a row.

**Seat height.** Listed heights are 45 cm and 50 cm. A lower lounger gives the room a calm, horizontal look; a slightly higher one is easier to get up from. If your hotel welcomes a lot of older guests, give this point some weight.

All models can be built to adjusted dimensions. If your architect has drawn a niche or a built-in plinth for the loungers, send us the drawing and we will check what is possible.

## Fixed back, adjustable back or flat body

This is the clearest dividing line in the range, and it is worth deciding early.

**Adjustable backrest.** On [Mercury](urun:SD-024), Moon and Odessa the back is raised by hand and goes up to 35°. Guests can lie flat or sit up to read. In hotel spas, where every guest seems to use the room differently, that flexibility pays for itself.

**Fixed backrest.** Evony, Light, Uranus and [Venus](urun:SD-031) have a fixed back with a back height of 85 cm. There is nothing to adjust and nothing for staff to reset, and a row of them always looks tidy. They suit busy day spas and hammams with a steady flow of guests.

**Flat body.** The [Marble](urun:SD-025) and [Mosaic](urun:SD-027) loungers have no cushioned back; the body itself is the surface. They are usually treated as part of the wet-area architecture rather than as loose furniture.

## Materials and finishes

**Timber and leather.** Evony, Light, Mercury, Moon, Odessa, Uranus and Venus use a moisture-resistant timber frame with leather upholstery. Cushions are specified at 10 cm thick with 32 DNS foam. These are the natural choice for dry lounges and for waiting areas next to treatment rooms.

**Marble.** The marble lounger shares its material language with a traditional hammam. It can be ordered with heating, so the stone surface stays warm to the touch. If you are planning a full hammam, it is worth reviewing the loungers together with our [hammam construction](hizmet:hamam) service so that services, drainage and finishes are coordinated.

**Glass mosaic.** The Mosaic lounger is also available with heating. Glass mosaic comes in a wide range of colours, which lets a designer carry a wall or floor pattern onto the furniture itself.

**Rattan.** The [Rattan lounger](urun:SD-029) is suitable for indoor and outdoor use, has a UV-filtered surface coating and is described as resistant to both cold and heat. Treat it as a separate line item for pool decks, terraces and winter gardens; a side table can be added.

## Colour choices

Guests spend more unhurried time looking at a relaxation room than at almost any other space in a spa, so colour should not be the last decision on the list.

On Mercury, Moon and Odessa both the timber and the leather colour can be specified. On Evony, Light, Uranus and Venus the leather colour is the variable. A sensible order is to fix the floor and wall finishes first and then choose the leather from within that palette. Pale leather opens a room up but shows marks sooner under heavy use; darker tones hide wear but can disappear in a dimly lit lounge. If you would like to see samples before signing off, tell us at the quotation stage.

## Housekeeping questions to settle early

We do not publish model-specific cleaning instructions here, but a few operational questions help you choose and specify correctly:

- Will guests sit down while still wet? If so, a towel-on-every-lounger rule or a protective cover makes sense.
- Which cleaning products will your housekeeping team use on leather? Ask us before handover so that the products match the upholstery.
- If you choose heated marble or mosaic, how will heating be switched on and off around opening hours?
- Where will rattan pieces be stored in poor weather?

Answering these usually settles the last open questions about model and options.

## Writing a clear specification

When you send an enquiry, a short brief saves several rounds of messages:

- room dimensions and a sketch or floor plan;
- number of loungers and preferred layout;
- back type (adjustable, fixed or flat body);
- material and colour direction;
- options per lounger;
- any loungers needed for outdoor or poolside areas.

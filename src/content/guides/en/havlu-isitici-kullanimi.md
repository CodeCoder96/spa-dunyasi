## Why a towel cabinet earns its place

In a busy treatment room, a warm towel is needed at predictable moments: removing oil at the end of a massage, covering the face between steps of a facial, wrapping the feet during a pedicure, or offering a hot towel in a barber's chair. Without a cabinet, the therapist leaves the guest to fetch one. With a cabinet in the right place, the towel is already within reach.

A cabinet also brings order to the working day. When everyone knows where clean towels wait, where used ones go and who refills the unit, the treatment schedule runs without small interruptions. This guide covers how to choose between the models we supply, where to put them and how to set up a loading routine your team can follow.

How warm a towel feels when it reaches the guest also depends on how often the door is opened, how thick the towel is and how long it has been inside. That is why the routine later in this guide asks therapists to check each towel by hand.

## Choosing between the models

With identical technical data, the differences are the name, the body style and the volume options. Three questions usually settle it.

**What does the room look like?** In a spa finished mainly in timber, a wooden cabinet blends in with the furniture; the [Wood Towel Warmer](urun:SD-047) is named for exactly that style of body. In a cleaner, more contemporary beauty salon, a classic cabinet body may sit more comfortably.

**Which volume do you need?** The Towel Warmer Cabinet, Towel Warmer and Wood models are listed at 23 litres. The [Steriliser Cabinet](urun:SD-045) is offered in 5 or 16 litre versions, or as a Mini. A Mini can suit a compact nail bar, while a larger volume makes more sense in a corridor serving several rooms.

## Placement in the room

Where the cabinet sits decides how many steps your team takes in a day. Consider the following when planning its position.

- **Power.** The unit runs from the mains. A dedicated socket at the planned spot is tidier and safer than an extension lead across the floor.
- **A firm, level base.** A worktop, shelf or trolley top is fine, provided it does not wobble and the cabinet cannot tip forward when the door is opened.
- **Distance from water.** Keep it away from basins, pedicure tubs and shower heads, with clear space between the wet zone and the unit.
- **Door clearance.** Leave room in front for the door to open fully and for the therapist to lift towels out comfortably.
- **Air space.** Leave a gap behind and beside the cabinet rather than pushing it against a wall or shutting it inside a cupboard. Follow the placement notes in the user manual.
- **Staff-only reach.** Position it where the therapist can reach it easily but guests are unlikely to touch it.

**Central station or one per room?** With only a few treatment rooms, a single cabinet in the corridor may be enough. If rooms are spread out, or if you run services such as facials where towels are changed several times, a cabinet in each room keeps therapists with their guests.

## How different services use the cabinet

The same unit is used at a different pace depending on the treatment menu.

- **Massage suites.** A cabinet on the top shelf of a bedside trolley lets the therapist reach a towel at the point where the treatment ends.
- **Facial rooms.** Towels are used several times during a protocol, so keep the cabinet close to the chair and route the cable away from the walkway.
- **Pedicure stations.** With water in constant use, it is especially important to keep the cabinet on a dry worktop, clear of the foot tub.
- **Barbers and hair salons.** Where several chairs share one cabinet, place it where every station can reach it and check stock frequently at busy times.
- **Hammam and heat-room exits.** Keep the cabinet in a dry transition area or near reception, never inside the wet zone itself.

## A loading routine your team can share

The routine below is a general framework. Your unit's user manual always takes priority.

1. **Opening up.** Check that the interior is clean and dry, then switch the cabinet on.
2. **Prepare the towels.** Use only laundered, fully dried towels. Rolling or folding them the same way makes loading and counting quicker.
3. **Do not overfill.** The listed capacity is an upper limit. Arrange towels loosely enough for the door to close without force.
4. **Keep the door shut.** Every time the door is left open, the interior cools. Take a towel and close it straight away.
5. **Rotate stock.** Add fresh towels at the back and take from the front, so the first towel in is the first towel out.
6. **Handle with care.** The therapist should check each towel's warmth in their own hands before offering it, using tongs where appropriate.
7. **Closing down.** Empty the cabinet, switch it off and unplug it. Wipe the interior as the manual recommends and leave the door ajar to dry.

A short instruction card fixed beside the cabinet helps new staff pick up the same habits from their first shift.

## Common mistakes

Most problems we hear about after opening come from habits rather than hardware:

- placing the cabinet right next to a pedicure tub or basin;
- enclosing it in a cupboard with no space around it;
- loading towels that are still damp from the dryer;
- leaving the door ajar throughout the day;
- not assigning anyone to keep it stocked;
- leaving at night without emptying and unplugging it.

Adding cabinet use to staff induction is the simplest way to avoid all of these.

Some spas offer a cold towel alongside the warm one, for instance after a sauna. Placing both units side by side makes the service simpler to run.

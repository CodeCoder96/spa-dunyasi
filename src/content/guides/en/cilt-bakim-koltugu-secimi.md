## What a treatment chair has to do

Facials, brow and lash work, waxing and semi-permanent make-up all share one feature: the therapist works close to the client, usually seated, and often for long stretches. A massage bed is built for the opposite situation, with the therapist standing and using body weight. That is why many salons and hotel spas end up specifying a dedicated chair for their beauty rooms even when they already own good massage beds.

A treatment chair has to do three things well. It must let the client move from sitting to reclining without getting up. It must bring the client's face or feet to a height that suits a seated therapist. And it must stay steady while fine work is being done. In our [treatment and pedicure chair range](kategori:bakim-ve-pedikur-koltuklari), the [Brand beauty treatment chair](urun:SD-032) and the [Podology care chair](urun:SD-034) are the two electric models designed around that brief. Both are described as having a sturdy frame that does not sway during treatment.

## Map your treatment menu first

Before comparing models, list every service the room will host. Then mark the client position each one needs:

- **Upright or semi-reclined:** consultation, skin analysis, brow and lash work.
- **Flat on the back:** facials, waxing, body treatments.
- **Prone:** back facials and some body work, which need a face hole.
- **Legs raised:** foot care and podology.

A room that only ever does one of these can live with a simpler setup. A room that switches between three or four, or is shared by therapists of different heights, will get daily use out of motorised adjustment.

## The three motorised movements

On both the Brand and Podology chairs, height, backrest and leg section are adjusted electrically by remote control.

**Height** lets each therapist set the client's face to a comfortable level relative to their own seated posture. In a room shared across shifts, this is the adjustment that gets used most.

**Backrest** moves the client from a conversational, semi-upright position into a flat working position without asking them to shift on the upholstery.

**Leg section** raises the client's legs, which helps them settle during longer appointments and, in foot care rooms, defines the working surface.

## The fourth motor and the Trendelenburg position

A fourth motor is offered as an option on both chairs. It adds the Trendelenburg position, in which the whole chair tilts so that the head end sits lower than the foot end. Some aesthetic and medical-aesthetic procedures specify this position. If yours do, include the fourth motor in your enquiry; if not, the standard three-motor setup covers everyday beauty work. We suggest you agree this point with the practitioner who will carry out the procedures before you order.

## Footprint and room planning

The Brand and Podology chairs are 77 cm wide and 180 cm long. Most massage beds in our range are 80 cm wide and 200 cm long. The saving is modest on paper, but in a compact beauty room it frees up the space behind the head end where the therapist sits with a stool and trolley.

Some practical layout points:

- Keep the head end clear: this is where the therapist, stool, lamp and device trolley will be.
- Leave one long side open so that clients can get on and off without squeezing past equipment.
- Plan power sockets near the head end so that cables never cross a walkway.
- Where possible, position the chair so that the foot end is not visible from the doorway.

The stated load capacity of the Brand and Podology chairs is 200 kg, compared with 350 kg for our massage beds. Take this into account if your client profile calls for it.

If your business is mainly massage with occasional facials, a single motorised massage bed may serve both purposes better than a separate chair.

## Upholstery, colour and hygiene routines

The leather colour of both chairs can be specified. Beauty rooms tend to be designed light and clean, yet very pale upholstery shows tint, oil and product marks quickly. Rooms used for brow tinting or semi-permanent make-up often do better with mid tones.

We do not set out model-specific cleaning instructions here, but these questions are worth agreeing internally:

- Will you use disposable couch roll or washable towels between clients?
- If you use the face hole, how will it be covered for each client?
- Which cleaning products will your team use on the upholstery? Ask us at handover so they match.
- If you choose the heating option, when will it be switched on during the working day?

## The rest of the workstation

**Stool.** Therapists spend most of a facial seated. Our [stool](urun:SD-062) runs on castors, is listed with a 50-65 cm height range and comes in backed, backless, saddle and flat-seat versions. A backed seat suits long facials; a saddle seat suits work that involves frequent leaning and turning.

**Magnifier lamp.** Close work needs light and magnification together. The [floor-standing magnifier lamp](urun:SD-056) combines 5X magnification with 90 LEDs and adjustable light levels, on a five-legged base that is easy to move around the chair.

**Heating and bolster.** Both are available as options on the Brand and Podology chairs. A bolster under the knees helps the client settle in the reclined position.

## Planning several rooms at once

For a new clinic or a hotel spa with several beauty rooms, consider grouping rooms by treatment rather than fitting the same piece everywhere: electric treatment chairs in facial and lash rooms, a motorised massage bed where body and face work are combined, and the Podology chair in a dedicated foot care room. Alternatively, standardising on one model brings its own benefits, since staff learn one remote and one set of positions, linen sizes stay uniform and the colour decision is made once.

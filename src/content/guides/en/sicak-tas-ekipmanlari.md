## What a hot stone station involves

Hot stone massage is one of the treatments spas and massage studios most often consider when they want to extend their menu. The technique itself depends on the therapist's training and the protocol your business follows. This guide looks only at the equipment side: what you need, how to choose it, where it goes in the room and how it fits into a working day.

A typical station has four parts:

- **A stone set** with pieces of different shapes and sizes.
- **A stone heater** that brings the stones up to temperature and keeps them there during the session.
- **Massage oil**, and a way to warm it if your protocol calls for that.
- **A treatment bed and trolley**, the bed for the guest and a mobile surface for the heater.

## Choosing the stones

Variety is the first thing to look for. Over a session, a therapist reaches for broad flat stones, palm-sized pieces and smaller stones for more detailed work, so a set with a good spread of sizes is more useful than many identical pieces.

## Setting up the room

The heater's position affects both the therapist's movement and safety in the room.

- **Beside the bed.** Stones are swapped frequently during a session. Keeping the heater on the therapist's working side saves steps.
- **On a stable surface.** Use the top shelf of a sturdy [spa trolley](urun:SD-059); the range includes beautician and Thai trolleys alongside other models. Castors let you position the heater to suit the room, but lock them during the treatment.
- **Power and cables.** The heater runs on mains power. Decide on the socket position in advance so the cable does not cross the therapist's walkway.
- **Away from the guest.** The lid and body become hot. Choose a spot the guest will not brush against when getting on or off the bed.
- **Splash-friendly surfaces.** Oil and water will occasionally spill, so put the heater on a surface that is quick to wipe clean.

## A session from start to finish

The steps below are a general outline. The heater's user manual and your therapists' training always take precedence.

1. **Preparation.** Place clean stones in the heater and prepare the unit as described in the manual.
2. **Temperature.** Choose a setting within the heating range that suits your protocol. Using the same setting every time keeps results consistent across the team.
3. **Hand check.** Before any stone touches the guest, the therapist checks it in their own hand. Make this a fixed step in training.
4. **During the session.** Return used stones to the heater with the wooden spoon or a suitable tool, and keep the lid closed between swaps.
5. **Finishing.** Remove the stones, switch the heater off and move on to cleaning.

## Cleaning and storage

Stones pick up oil in every session, so a clear routine keeps the kit looking good and ready for the next guest.

- Remove oil from the stones after each session and dry them thoroughly.
- Clean the heater basin as the manual recommends, without letting oil residue build up.
- Store stones in a dry, closed box or drawer, arranged so they do not knock against each other.
- Set aside any stone that has cracked or whose surface has deteriorated.

Pinning the routine to the wall of the treatment room helps when several therapists share the same equipment.

## Business decisions before launch

Buying the kit is only part of adding hot stones to the menu. Settle these points as well.

**Dedicated room or shared?** A dedicated room is not essential, but one where the heater, stones and trolley stay in place avoids setting up before every booking. If the kit moves between rooms, a trolley on castors makes that easier.

**Appointment spacing.** Cleaning and reheating stones takes preparation time between consecutive hot stone bookings. Without a spare set, build that into the schedule.

**Team training.** Every therapist using the kit should handle temperature settings, stone checks and cleaning the same way. A short hands-on introduction for new staff reduces variation.

**Guest information.** A brief, plain description of how the treatment is carried out, shared at booking, helps answer questions before the guest arrives.

**Related treatments.** If your menu also includes treatments with warmed sand or salt, products such as the Trend Salt & Sand Bed can be planned alongside the hot stone station.

## The decision in one paragraph

If one therapist does one kind of treatment in a room, a fixed bed set to the right height is usually enough. If several therapists share the room, add a motor so each can set their own working height. If the room combines massage with facials or body treatments, look at a three-motor bed that also moves the backrest and leg section. And if you offer Thai massage, head massage or heavily oil-based rituals, start with the specialist frames. Size, for most operators, is the easy part. The rest of this guide explains the reasoning so that you can brief your design team or procurement department with confidence.

## The range at a glance

Our [massage bed range](kategori:masaj-yataklari) covers 21 models. Of those with listed dimensions, most are 80 cm wide and every one is 200 cm long. The stated load capacity across them is 350 kg. Where cushion thickness is specified it is 10 cm, and foam density is 30 DNS on most models and 28 DNS on the head massage bed.

In other words, what separates one model from another is rarely the footprint. It is the frame design, the motor configuration, the materials and the options.

## Fixed frames: simple and dependable

A fixed bed has no moving parts and nothing to plug in. It is a sound choice when:

- the same therapist works in the room every day and the height can be set for them at the ordering stage;
- almost every treatment is carried out with the guest lying flat;
- there is no convenient power socket near the bed, or you would rather keep cables out of the room.

The fixed versions of [Classic](urun:SD-007), Trend and [Space](urun:SD-018) fit this profile. They share a face hole, a moisture-resistant timber frame and colour choices for both timber and leather. Because the height of a fixed bed is set when you order, talk to the therapist who will use it before you finalise the specification.

## One or two motors: flexibility for shared rooms

The same Classic, Trend and Space frames are available with one or two motors. In a hotel spa where morning and evening shifts are staffed by different therapists, a motorised version lets each person bring the bed to their own working height rather than adapting their posture to the furniture.

We recommend you confirm at the quotation stage exactly which movement each motor provides, so that you know in advance whether height and backrest are motorised or manual on the configuration you choose.

## Three motors: one bed, several treatments

[Lux Soft](urun:SD-013), Soft and [Nice](urun:SD-014) use three motors. Height, backrest and leg section are all adjusted electrically by remote control. On Lux Soft and Soft the backrest rises to 35° and the leg section to 25°, and the armrests fold down on pressure springs. All three have a curved body.

A three-motor bed behaves more like a workstation than a table. It lies flat for a full-body massage and moves into a semi-reclined position for a facial without the guest needing to get up. For spas with a limited number of rooms and a broad treatment menu, that versatility is the main argument. ## Working height and therapist posture

Working height is the factor most often overlooked by buyers and most often mentioned by therapists. Too low, and the therapist bends forward for hours; too high, and they lift their shoulders to apply pressure.

In our range, most models list a height of 68-85 cm. Avant and Ayurveda list 50 cm, and the Thai bed sits at 10-20 cm. Models whose height is adjusted by remote say so in their equipment list.

A practical approach is to send us the heights of the therapists who will use the room and the treatments they do most. A room used mainly for deep tissue work does not need the same height as one used mainly for facial massage. Where therapist heights vary widely, a motorised version protects you against staffing changes.

## Size and room clearance

A surface 80 cm wide and 200 cm long balances two needs: the guest can rest their arms comfortably at their sides, and the therapist can still reach across to the far side. When planning a room, keep clear space along both long sides and at the head end. A bed pushed against a wall forces the therapist to work from one side, which makes many techniques awkward.

Avant, Ayurveda and the Thai bed can be built to adjusted dimensions. That is useful when a room is irregular or when the architect has already drawn a fixed position for the bed.

## Frame material and storage

Two construction approaches run through the range. Classic, Trend, Space and the three-motor models use a moisture-resistant timber frame. The Base series, including [Base X](urun:SD-005), uses an MDF body resistant to water and moisture, with a lower compartment for pillows, oils, towels and peshtemals. In small rooms where a separate cabinet will not fit, that compartment does a lot of work. A manual back lift is offered as an option on the Base models.

Timber and leather colours can be chosen separately on many models. Deciding them against the room's floor and wall finishes is the simplest way to avoid a mismatch later.

## Choosing options

Most models can be ordered with heating, a bolster, a protective cover, a drawer and a segmented cushion. The heating option is listed as 220V/180W. A protective cover is worth considering in rooms with heavy oil use, and a drawer keeps products and towels within reach. Valid options for each model are listed on its product page.

## A buyer's checklist

1. List the treatments the room will host.
2. Note who will work in it: one therapist or a rotating team.
3. Decide whether guests always lie flat or need the back and legs raised.
4. Check that the room leaves clearance around a standard bed.
5. Decide whether you need built-in storage.
6. Choose materials, colours and options per bed.

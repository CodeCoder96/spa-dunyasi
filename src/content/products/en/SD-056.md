## Seeing the detail

Skin analysis, brow and lash work and detailed facials all require the practitioner to look at the face from very close range. At that distance, ceiling lights tend to cast shadows, and the therapist's own head often blocks what light there is. A magnifier lamp solves both problems at once: the lens enlarges the area 5X, while the LED ring directs light straight onto the spot being worked on.

Being able to dim or brighten the light means one lamp can serve several services. A softer setting suits work close to the guest's eyes; a brighter one helps when examining the skin surface.

Magnification earns its place when the detail is hard to pick out with the naked eye: fine hairs, pore texture or the small area around a nail. Because the therapist no longer has to lean in so far, it is also easier to keep a good posture through a long session, and guests see a room that looks careful and professional.

## Why a floor-standing model?

Not every cabin has a convenient desk or wall for a clamp-on lamp. A model on its own base stays independent of the room layout. You can pull it to the head of the bed for a treatment and park it in a corner afterwards, and if you run several rooms the same lamp can move between them as bookings require.

When planning the room, leave a spot where the base will not clash with the legs of the chair or bed. That way the lens can be brought over the guest without forcing the base into position.

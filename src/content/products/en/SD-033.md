## Making room for the Light in your floor plan

A pedicure chair is not something you push against a wall and forget. It needs space around it for the therapist's stool, for supplies and for guests to get in and out comfortably. Think of the Light as a single station rather than a piece of seating. When drawing up the plan, leave room in front for the therapist and at the side for a second person doing the manicure.

Keeping the chair away from doorways and the busy route past reception helps guests settle during their appointment. Spots in front of a window or a wall, with nobody walking behind, tend to work well.

If several Light chairs will stand in a row, allow enough space between them for therapists to move without bumping into each other. The chair stands 110 cm high, so check whether any shelving or cabinets you plan to put behind it rise above that line. Send us a rough sketch showing doors and windows and we can work out together how many stations will fit. It also pays to discuss at this stage how the foot spa basin will be filled and emptied, so there are no small surprises after opening.

## The foot spa basin and working height

The Light has a spa basin where guests rest their feet. Preparing the feet and carrying out the pedicure happen in one place, without moving the guest anywhere else.

Height adjustment for the therapist matters in daily use too. Bending over for a long pedicure is tiring; when the chair can be brought to the right level for the person working, the day runs more smoothly.

## Manicure and pedicure together

The Light is set up so that hands and feet can be looked after in the same session. At busy times, two staff can work side by side and shorten the guest's visit. If you are planning this kind of layout, have a look at our [manicure tables](kategori:manikur) for the table beside the chair.

## Scent as part of the session

One of the first things a guest notices on entering a massage room is how it smells. Like lighting, music and the way towels are laid out, fragrance shapes the character of a venue, and the oil you use carries that character through the whole session.

The range covers seven scents. Rose, jasmine, chamomile and lavender suit those who like floral notes; orange gives a brighter fragrance; green tea is a light, fresh option; sandalwood brings a warmer, woody tone. A practical way to find the right one is to order a few together and try them with your team and regular guests before committing to larger quantities.

## Giving guests a choice

Some venues use one signature scent in every room, so guests come to associate the spa with that fragrance. Others ask the guest to pick from two or three oils before the session begins. Seven options leave room for either approach, and switching scents with the seasons or for special packages is an easy way to keep the menu feeling fresh.

## Planning it as a consumable

Because massage oil is used every working day, stock planning matters. Base a regular ordering rhythm on the number of rooms you run and how busy they are, so you are not caught short on a full day. Buying consumables from the same place as your equipment also keeps ordering and invoicing simpler.

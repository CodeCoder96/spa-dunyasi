## Why therapists keep bolsters to hand

However well upholstered a massage bed is, a flat surface does not fit every guest or every treatment. Therapists fill that gap with bolsters. Placed in the right spot, a cushion helps the guest settle into a steady position for a long session and gives the therapist easier access to the area being worked on.

This page covers a family of five shapes rather than a single cushion, and each one answers a different need on the bed.

## The five shapes

**Neck:** goes under the head and neck when the guest lies on their back. It is a common choice for facials and décolleté work.

**Cylinder:** long and round, used under the knees, or under the ankles when the guest lies face down.

**Half-moon:** used in much the same places as the cylinder; its half-round section lets it sit on its flat side on the bed.

**Triangle:** a sloping surface for moments when the back or legs need to be raised at an angle.

**Chest support:** shaped so that female guests can lie face down in a more comfortable position.

## Building a set for your rooms

Cylinders and half-moons suit almost any massage room, while a neck cushion earns its keep in facial cabins. The chest support and triangle tend to be added by venues with a broad treatment menu. In a busy spa it is also handy to keep a spare set, so one can be cleaned while the other stays in use.

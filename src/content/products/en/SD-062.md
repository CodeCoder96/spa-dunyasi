## Where the therapist sits

In a treatment room, attention usually goes to the furniture the guest sits or lies on. Yet for a practitioner who spends hours seated while doing facials, manicures or pedicures, the stool is a core working tool they share most of the day with. A well-chosen stool helps them work close to the guest in a balanced position.

Castors let the therapist change position without standing up. Moving from the head of the bed to the side during a facial, or sliding closer to the foot bath during a pedicure, becomes a smooth movement, and the session is not interrupted by constant getting up and sitting down.

The stool is not limited to beauty cabins. It works just as well at a manicure table, for brow and lash work, or in a massage room when the therapist is working on the face and scalp. In venues offering several services, keeping one stool in every room saves staff from carrying furniture between rooms.

## Seat types

There are four seat types to choose from:

- **With backrest:** for therapists who like to lean back during long sessions.
- **Without backrest:** a simple option that takes up less space and can be sat on from any side.
- **Saddle:** a seat shaped like a riding saddle, for those who prefer to lean in towards the guest.
- **Flat:** a classic level seat.

The choice depends entirely on personal working habits. If your team has different preferences, you can order a different version for each room.

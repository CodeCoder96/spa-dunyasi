## How a pedicure appointment flows

A guest's experience of a pedicure is often spoilt by small transitions: soaking feet in a separate bowl, moving to another seat, carrying towels across the room. The Spa model gathers these steps in one spot. The guest sits down, places their feet in the spa basin and stays put until the work is done.

That flow helps the therapist as well. With supplies arranged around the chair, there is no need to keep getting up and sitting down, and the appointment moves at a calmer, more predictable pace.

Keeping towels, files and products on a trolley or shelf within arm's reach completes the picture. Building a few minutes into each booking slot for emptying and cleaning the basin lets the next guest sit down without waiting, which keeps the salon on schedule at busy weekends.

## Why the therapist's posture matters

For someone doing several pedicures back to back, working height is no small detail. On the Spa model, the chair height can be brought to the level the therapist finds comfortable. In salons where staff of different heights share one chair, this lets each shift set it up for the person on duty.

## Who chooses the Spa model?

Beauty salons that lead with pedicures, hotel spas and boutique hand-and-foot studios are all good candidates. Because the chair also lets someone attend to the guest's hands, businesses selling manicure and pedicure as a package can have two staff working at once.

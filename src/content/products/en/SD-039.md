## Table and chair: two halves of one station

What shapes guest comfort and technician output in a manicure service is less the table itself than the relationship between the table and the seating. With the table at a fixed height, a seat that is too low or too high forces the technician to raise their shoulders or lean forward. That is why we suggest planning the chair at the same time as the Light table.

The manicure chair in our catalogue rises and lowers on a hydraulic mechanism. When the table stays at a fixed height and the chair can be set for guest or technician, people of different heights can all use the same station comfortably.

## How many stations?

Whether you are opening a new salon or expanding an existing one, the number of stations should follow from your floor space. Allow for each table, two seats facing each other and comfortable walkways in between. A row of Light tables gives the salon a tidy, uniform look. Lining them along a wall is one option; facing islands in the middle of the room make it easier for guests to see and chat with each other. Whichever you choose, leave room on the technician's side for a supply trolley or cabinet. If you will use electric lamps and dryers, sockets aligned with the tables keep cables out of walkways.

Some salons place the manicure table right next to a pedicure chair instead of in a separate corner, so the manicure and pedicure can be done at once. If you want that kind of layout, consider the [Spa Life pedicure chair](urun:SD-035) together with the Light table.

## Colour and size

You can pick the wood colour to suit the overall look of your salon. If your space does not suit the standard size, the table's dimensions can be adapted on request; sending your floor plan speeds this up.

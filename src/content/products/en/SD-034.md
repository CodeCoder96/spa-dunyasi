## What the chair does in a podology practice

Most podology work happens at the client's feet, on fine detail. The practitioner usually sits on a stool and wants the feet close to eye level. A chair with limited adjustment does not always allow this; when it stays in one position, the practitioner ends up bending their own posture to fit it.

Here, the chair adapts to the practitioner instead. The leg section rises from the hand control to bring the feet to working level, the backrest moves to an angle the client finds comfortable and, if needed, the whole chair goes up or down. Lowering it while the client sits down or stands up also makes daily routines easier.

## A steady base for precise work

When working with foot-care instruments, a chair that does not wobble matters. The frame is built to stay steady during treatments. A 200 kg load capacity means you can work with clients of all builds in the same chair. The 77 cm width gives a frame the practitioner can approach from either side; when arranging the room, remember to leave enough space at the foot end for a stool and an instrument trolley.

## Configured to your needs

The heating option adds to client comfort in cool treatment rooms. A bolster can support the legs or knees. The 4th motor tilts the chair into the Trendelenburg position, where the head end sits lower than the feet. A face hole is also on the option list, worth considering if your practice offers other services too.

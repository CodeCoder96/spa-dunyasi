## Pool projects for businesses

In hotels, holiday resorts, residential complexes and spas, the pool is a heavily used shared space. Beyond the shape of the pool itself, these projects need to consider how people reach it, where the showers and changing rooms are, how much room the poolside lounging area takes up and how staff will carry out maintenance. For indoor pools, the hall's humidity and ventilation and its link to other wet areas such as the hammam, sauna and steam room are also part of the plan.

To complete the poolside and changing areas, the same project can include the [Rattan Sun Lounger](urun:SD-029), which works indoors and outdoors, a [Lounge Side Table](urun:SD-058) beside it, timber-framed loungers such as [Uranus](urun:SD-030) for an indoor pool hall, and a [Swimsuit Dryer](urun:SD-061) and [Towel Cooler](urun:SD-044) for the changing rooms.

## Indoor or outdoor?

Whether a pool is indoors or outdoors affects almost every detail. Outdoors, the direction of the sun, wind, trees and landscaping steer the design, and the pool is planned together with a generous terrace and lounging area. Indoors, ventilation, humidity control, ceiling and wall materials and lighting come to the fore. In many hotel and spa projects the indoor pool is planned on the same floor as the sauna, hammam and steam room, as one connected wet area. We will help you decide which option suits you after hearing about your space and how you intend to use it.

## The poolside and changing area

How comfortable a pool is to use depends on the space around the water. Showers close to the pool entrance, a short, non-slip route from the changing room and a shaded or covered rest corner on the poolside all shape how people experience it. We plan these areas on the same drawings as the pool.

## Home and villa pools

For a home pool, the priority is usually making good use of the garden or basement. How the pool looks from the house, when it gets the sun, children's safety and where the poolside seating will go are the main design questions. If you are planning an indoor pool at home, ventilation and the link to the other rooms need to be addressed from the start.

At a villa, the visual link between the pool and the house also matters. How the pool looks from the living room or terrace, the mood its night lighting gives the garden and how much room is left for dining or seating by the water are all topics we cover in design meetings. The finish colour changes the visible tone of the water, so we choose it with samples.

## Preparing for the project

To make the first conversation productive, we suggest gathering:

- **Size and location:** the dimensions of the area set aside for the pool, any site plan or architectural drawings, and its distance from the building and the boundaries.
- **Site conditions:** the ground, any slope, existing water and electrical lines, space for a plant room and whether machinery can reach the site.
- **Intended use:** swimming, relaxing or children's play; indoor or outdoor; and, for a business pool, how many people will use it each day.

Sending pictures of pools you like along with this information helps us understand what you have in mind.

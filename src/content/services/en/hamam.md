## Hotel and spa hammams

In many hotels the hammam is the heart of the spa. Guests leave the changing room, pass through a warm transition space into the hot room and, after the scrub and foam ritual, move on to the rest area. To keep that sequence smooth, we plan the hammam on the same drawings as the sauna, steam room, showers and relaxation area.

Another point for commercial hammams is how many attendants or therapists work at once. The size of the heated platform, the position of the basins and any private scrub rooms follow from that number. For the rest area after the hammam, the [Marble Relaxation Lounger](urun:SD-025) or the glass-faced [Mosaic Relaxation Lounger](urun:SD-027) echo the materials of the hammam itself. If you want to offer wet treatments, the [Mosaic and Marble Vichy Shower](urun:SD-053) or the timber-framed [Wood Vichy Shower](urun:SD-055) can be planned into the same project.

## Villa and home hammams

A home hammam does not have to be a scaled-down copy of a commercial one. In most homes a single heated platform or seating ledge, one or two basins and an entrance linked to the bathroom are enough. What matters most is separating the hammam properly from the rest of the house in terms of heat and humidity, and making it easy to clean. For colours and finishes, we take the overall style of your home as the starting point.

## Materials, colour and light

Surfaces shape the character of a hammam. Veined marble gives a classic look, while coloured glass mosaic feels brighter and more contemporary. When the finishes chosen for the platform, basins and seating ledges work together, the room reads as one space. Small light openings in the dome or vault, concealed lighting at floor level and accent lights around the basins give the room a calm atmosphere. We review these choices with sample surfaces and images during design.

## Planning the hammam with the surrounding spaces

A hammam never works in isolation. The routes to the changing rooms, showers, rest area and any massage rooms decide where guests go next. Non-slip surfaces and well-detailed thresholds between wet and dry floors also make the area easier to run day to day. That is why, in most projects, we put the hammam at the centre of the wet area plan.

## What to prepare before the project

Because a hammam is closely tied to plumbing and building work, the information we need is more detailed than for a sauna or a furniture order:

1. **Measurements and drawings:** the floor plan, ceiling height and any architectural drawings. If you have none, a measured sketch and photos from several angles.
2. **Condition of the space:** new build or renovation, where water, drainage and electrical lines come in, the floor level and the position of structural elements.
3. **Intended use:** whether the hammam will serve hotel guests, spa clients or only your family, and whether scrub and foam treatments will take place there.
4. **Look and feel:** a classic Turkish hammam or a simpler, contemporary take. Images of examples you like help a great deal.

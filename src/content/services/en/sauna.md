## Sauna projects for businesses

Hotel spas, wellness centres, gyms and residential leisure facilities all use a sauna for different reasons. In a hotel the sauna is part of the guest's stay, so it is planned in the same flow as the hammam, steam room or pool. In a gym the cabin is used all day by a steady stream of members, which puts the entrance and exit, the distance to the changing rooms and ease of cleaning at the top of the list.

For a commercial sauna, the cabin's capacity should be set so that members are not left queuing at the door at busy times. Tell us about your visitor numbers and opening hours, and we will size the cabin around them.

We also recommend planning the space around the cabin, not just the cabin itself. A rest area where guests can lie down afterwards, a spot where towels are kept ready and easy access to showers all shape the experience. For the rest area we can include timber-framed loungers such as [Mercury](urun:SD-024) or the roomier [Odessa](urun:SD-028), along with a [towel warmer cabinet](urun:SD-043) and a [towel cooler](urun:SD-044), in the same project.

## Saunas for homes

In a home the priority is usually making good use of the space available. A spare room next to a bathroom, a basement or an area beside the main en suite can all be considered. The cabin is sized around how many people in the household will use it and how often. Appearance matters too: whether the door is glazed or timber, the tone of the interior lighting and how the cabin sits with the rest of the house are decided together at the design stage.

## Decisions we make together at the design stage

Choices that look small on paper make a clear difference to daily use. In design meetings we work through the following:

- **Seating:** single-row benches or tiered seating, and whether people will mostly sit or lie down.
- **Door and glazing:** how visible the cabin should be from outside, which way the door opens and how it relates to the corridor or changing room.
- **Lighting:** soft, indirect light inside or enough light to read by, and whether the outside of the cabin has accent lighting.
- **Timber tone and details:** the colour of the cladding, the shape of the backrests and how the cabin fits the rest of the room.

Before manufacturing begins, we review the layout with you one final time, so there are fewer last-minute changes on site and the cabin is delivered looking the way you planned.

## What to prepare before the project starts

Having the following ready makes the first conversation far more productive:

- **Measurements:** the width, length and ceiling height of the room, plus the position of doors, windows and any columns. A simple hand sketch and a few photos are usually enough.
- **The space:** whether it is a new build or a renovation, the state of the electrical supply, ventilation options and the type of floor.
- **Intended use:** whether guests, members or only your family will use the sauna, how many people you expect inside at once and how busy it will be each day.

Once these three points are clear, we can talk realistically about cabin size, seating and heater choice. If you are still deciding between a sauna, a hammam and a steam room, our guide to [how they differ](rehber:hamam-sauna-buhar) will help.
